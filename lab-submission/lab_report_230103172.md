# 2. Phase 1: Forensic Diagnosis of False Sharing and Cache-Line Contention

## 2.1 Experimental Objective

The objective of this phase was to investigate false sharing in multiprocessing shared memory.

False sharing happens when different processes update separate variables located on the same CPU cache line. Although the variables are different, cache coherence mechanisms can cause additional communication between CPU cores.

The experiment used:

- Shared memory through `multiprocessing.shared_memory`.
- 64-bit unsigned integer values.
- Multiple worker processes.
- Different memory strides.
- 24,000,000 total operations.

The tested stride values were 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32.

## 2.2 Experiment 1A: Stride Sensitivity

The first experiment used 8 processes and tested six different memory strides.

| Stride | Separation (Bytes) | Median Time (ms) | IQR (ms) | Throughput (Mops/s) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 8 | 832.972 | 35.647 | 28.812 |
| 2 | 16 | 831.774 | 87.735 | 28.854 |
| 4 | 32 | 772.437 | 111.278 | 31.070 |
| 8 | 64 | 807.157 | 41.512 | 29.734 |
| 16 | 128 | 816.506 | 56.266 | 29.394 |
| 32 | 256 | 844.872 | 30.787 | 28.407 |

*Note: Throughput values are expressed in millions of operations per second (Mops/s).*

The best execution time was observed at stride 4, with a median time of 772.437 ms.

The results do not show a clear and consistent performance improvement at the 64-byte boundary. Therefore, the exact cache-line boundary was not conclusively identified by this experiment.

## 2.3 Experiment 1B: Process Scaling with Stride = 1

The second experiment investigated how execution time changes when the number of processes increases.

The first scaling experiment used stride 1.

| Processes (p) | Median Time (ms) | Speedup | Efficiency | Karp-Flatt |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 4270.922 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | N/A |
| 2 | 2181.664 | 1.9576 | 0.9788 | 0.021636 |
| 4 | 1213.069 | 3.5208 | 0.8802 | 0.045373 |
| 8 | 875.603 | 4.8777 | 0.6097 | 0.091446 |
| 16 | 794.495 | 5.3756 | 0.3360 | 0.131759 |

The results show that execution time decreased as the number of processes increased.

The speedup reached 5.3756 when using 16 processes. However, parallel efficiency decreased to 33.60%.

The Karp-Flatt metric increased from 0.021636 at 2 processes to 0.131759 at 16 processes. This indicates increasing parallel overhead and reduced scalability.

## 2.4 Experiment 1B: Process Scaling with Stride = 16

The second scaling experiment used stride 16, providing 128 bytes of separation between process slots.

| Processes (p) | Median Time (ms) | Speedup | Efficiency | Karp-Flatt |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 4476.469 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | N/A |
| 2 | 2136.730 | 2.0950 | 1.0475 | -0.045350 |
| 4 | 1159.320 | 3.8613 | 0.9653 | 0.011975 |
| 8 | 824.575 | 5.4288 | 0.6786 | 0.067660 |
| 16 | 799.716 | 5.5976 | 0.3498 | 0.123892 |

The stride 16 experiment showed a speedup of 5.5976 with 16 processes.

Compared with stride 1, the execution time at 8 processes was lower. However, at 16 processes, the difference was small.

The Karp-Flatt metric also increased as the number of processes increased, showing that parallel overhead becomes more significant at higher process counts.

## 2.5 Karp-Flatt Analysis

The Karp-Flatt metric estimates the effective serial fraction and parallel overhead.

The results show that both configurations experience increasing overhead as the process count grows.

For stride 1, the Karp-Flatt value increased from 0.021636 to 0.131759.

For stride 16, it increased from 0.011975 at 4 processes to 0.123892 at 16 processes.

The negative value at 2 processes for stride 16 indicates a measured speedup slightly above the ideal linear speedup. This can occur because of measurement variation and differences in execution conditions.

## 2.6 Phase 1 Conclusion

The experiment demonstrated that multiprocessing can significantly reduce execution time.

The highest measured speedup was 5.5976 with stride 16 and 16 processes.

However, the results did not demonstrate a sharp performance transition at exactly 64 bytes. The experiment also showed that increasing the number of processes does not guarantee proportional performance improvements.

The decreasing parallel efficiency at higher process counts indicates the presence of additional overhead.
